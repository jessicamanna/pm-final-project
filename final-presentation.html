1 · Module 1
Problem Hook & Value Proposition

Problem Hook & Value Proposition, Module 1

  • Scenario: Option C (instructor approved): Nomi, a B2B product sold to mortgage lenders that gives their retail loan officers fast, compliant marketing collateral

Strategic crisis

If lenders do nothing for the next 12 months:

  • Mortgage rates keep climbing. The 30-year fixed hit 7.40% on Oct 8, 2026, the highest since November 2023, and origination forecasts keep getting cut.
  • Margins stay thin. Independent lenders earned 25 bp per loan in Q2 2026, against a 39 bp average since 2008, and 60% of retail cost to originate is sales cost.
  • Headcount keeps coming down. Lenders are cutting staff, so lenders' marketing and creative teams shrink or hold flat while LOs fight harder for fewer purchase deals.
  • The production queue stays slow. A flyer takes about 4 hours of work but about a week to arrive, because it passes through briefing, design, compliance, and revisions across several people.
  • Loan officers stop asking. They don't submit requests they know will arrive too late, so the lender pays for a process that only works for planned requests.
  • More loan officers make their own materials, which puts non-compliant flyers on the street with the lender's name on them.
  • The channel at risk is the main one. Realtor and builder referrals drive most retail purchase volume, and purchase was 80% of independent lenders' volume in Q2 2026.

Moment of misery

A realtor finally takes the loan officer's call and ends it with "send me something." Partners remember who responds the same day, but the approved process takes about a week. The loan officer currently does one of three things:

1. She submits a request and waits. The flyer arrives after the moment has passed, or she gives up and cancels it.

2. She makes it herself in Canva, Word, or a generic AI tool. It's off-brand, often missing the NMLS ID, the Equal Housing logo, or required rate disclosures, and it goes straight to a referral partner.

3. She sends nothing. The door she just opened quietly closes.

The pain is concentrated in unplanned requests. Planned work, ordered a week ahead, mostly gets through the queue.

Problem hook

When a referral partner says "send me something," a loan officer has about a day to show she's the professional worth sending business to, and her lender's process takes about a week. With rates back above 7% and independent lenders earning 25 bp per loan, every realtor and builder relationship counts. But the only approved path runs through briefing, design, compliance, and revisions across several people. So for requests she can't plan ahead, she either makes her own materials, often missing required disclosures, or sends nothing. Either way the lender loses: the partner moves to another loan officer, or a non-compliant flyer goes out with the lender's name on it. Lenders can't hire their way out while they're cutting costs. Today no path is both fast enough to keep the door open and safe enough to carry the lender's name.

Value proposition

For retail branch loan officers, starting with top producers, who need to prove they're credible the moment a referral partner gives them an opening, Nomi delivers professional, on-brand, compliance-reviewed collateral that looks like theirs, fast enough to use while the door is still open, because today they must wait about a week, make their own non-compliant materials, or send nothing, and with rates above 7% and margins at 25 bp, every partner who moves to another loan officer is purchase volume the lender doesn't get back.

Cold-read self-review

The stakes for lenders are high enough to justify buying Nomi, and the moment of misery is specific and recurring, not an edge case. The weakest point is whether lenders will buy Nomi, and who at the lender signs. Time saved by the lender's marketing team doesn't show up as a hard cost cut, and a buyer hears "time saved" as "cut designers," so the case has to rest on referral revenue instead. That revenue lift is still unproven, so referrals from partners tagged in the brief are the lagging North Star, and the pilot's primary metric is the leading indicator: urgent partner requests answered with a reviewed asset within 1 business day, measured with a 90-day A/B test. Two other risks stay open: lenders' compliance teams may not trust AI-only review on rates and co-branding, and templates that look alike could recreate the problem faster.

4 · Module 5
Experimentation Plan

Overview

We will validate the hypothesis with a 90-day A/B test at one lender, splitting the lender's 30 top-producing loan officers 50/50 between Nomi and today's process for urgent partner requests for product flyers. Before launch we lock the primary metric, the guardrail and its boundary, the smallest lift worth shipping, and a fixed read date, so the go/no-go call follows a rule written now, not how the results look later. Nomi is the only difference between the groups: the lender's request queue, the weekly LO check-in, brand templates, the partner CRM, and LO compensation stay the same for both.

What you're testing

Element · Detail

Hypothesis under test · I believe that giving top-producing loan officers Nomi (chat brief → draft → compliance check → ready-to-send product flyer) for urgent partner requests will move those requests back into a compliant path, as measured by an increase from 14% to 60% in urgent partner requests answered with a reviewed asset within 1 business day within 90 days. We will protect the post-delivery compliance issue rate throughout the test.

Experiment design (A/B, etc.) · 50/50 A/B test behind a feature flag: the lender's 30 top producers are randomly split into 15 who get Nomi (variant) and 15 who keep today's process (control) for 90 days. We randomize by LO, not by request, so each LO's experience stays consistent. We considered two other designs. A pre/post pilot with all 30 LOs has no control group, so a change in the lender's queue staffing or the market during the pilot could not be separated from Nomi's effect (losing two production staff alone moved urgent on-time delivery from 34% to 19%). A staggered rollout in three waves leaves a shrinking control group, about 70 control requests over 90 days, too few to read once each LO's requests are counted together. The 50/50 split gives each group about 100 urgent requests (15 LOs × about 2.3 a month × 3 months). Sample: 48 requests per arm at a 14% baseline and a +25 pt minimum lift (power 80%, significance 5%), or about 77 once each LO's requests are treated as related, so about 100 per arm covers it. Limitation: with 30 LOs, the test can confirm a large lift but not a small one. Control LOs get Nomi at the read date if it ships.

Primary metric · % of urgent partner requests (all channels: Nomi briefs, lender queue tickets, weekly LO check-in) answered with a compliance-reviewed asset within 1 business day, compared between the Nomi and control groups. Baseline 14%, target 60%, minimum worth shipping +25 pts (39%). Referral revenue is not the primary metric because it can't be read inside the test: the P90 time from lead to funding for a purchase loan is 164 days, well past the 90-day read date. It is also too rare to test at pilot scale: only a fraction of flyers lead to a referral, and only about 7% of referrals close and fund, so a significant difference in funded loans would take far more assets than 30 LOs send in 90 days. Referred applications and funded loans from partners tagged in the brief remain the lagging North Star, tracked for both groups and reviewed after the test.

Guardrail metric · Post-delivery compliance issue rate, from a 10% sampling audit of delivered assets with rates or trigger terms. Boundary: no higher in the Nomi group than for the control group's approved assets. Because the lender's compliance team holds the veto, a breach at any point pauses Nomi through the feature flag; this is a safety stop, not an early read of the primary metric.

Decision rule (ship / kill / pivot) · Ship if the Nomi group's rate beats the control by at least 25 pts at p < 0.05 and the compliance issue rate stays within its boundary at day 90: roll out to all of the lender's top producers, with new LOs next. Iterate if the Nomi group is ahead but by less than 25 pts: sharpen the chat brief or templates, or add a second lender for more sample, and re-run. Investigate if the lift clears 25 pts but the guardrail breaks, or results get worse for one branch or partner type: find who was harmed before any rollout. Kill if the Nomi group shows no improvement over the control or does worse. The read date is fixed at day 90, and no results are reviewed before then.

Findings & decision (after running it)

Not run yet. The test is planned with one lender: 30 top producers randomized 15/15 on day 0, with the read on day 90.

GTM Launch Plan & Success Dashboard

Go-to-market summary

We will launch Nomi's Smart Brief (one chat to a compliance-reviewed, on-brand product flyer in about 3 minutes) to independent retail mortgage lenders with a conversion goal: turn the pilot lender's results into 5 signed lenders within 90 days of launch. The audience is the person who signs at the lender, the head of marketing or head of sales, plus the lender's CEO, with the lender's compliance lead and General Counsel reached as the veto holders. This is an L, multi-channel launch: a new product entering a defined B2B market, but one lender's pilot doesn't yet justify an XL campaign. Jan 6, 2027 is a release to the pilot lender, not the launch; the launch moment follows the day-90 read of the A/B test in April 2027, and only if Nomi clears the ship bar (at least +25 pts on urgent requests answered with a reviewed asset within 1 business day, with no rise in compliance issues). Channels: owned, founder-led outreach to lender contacts, organic LinkedIn campaigns, and the Nomi website with the clickable demo; earned, the pilot lender's case study and reference calls, PR with mortgage trade press such as HousingWire, and a speaking slot at the Digital Mortgage conference; paid, full-funnel UGC ads on X driving demo requests, because lender CEOs are active there, and a CEO who wants Nomi collapses the slow pace of enterprise sales. Enablement: a buyer one-pager built on referral revenue, not cost savings; a compliance pack for the lender's GC (audit trail, ruleset, 10% sampling audit); the pilot case study; an objection sheet ("Will our GC approve AI review?", "Will flyers look alike?", "Is this cutting designers?"); and an LO onboarding guide. Owners: Jessica Manna (founder) owns the launch plan, founder-led sales, PR, and speaking; the designer owns the case study, one-pager, and ad creative; the compliance advisor owns the compliance pack; the engineers own lender onboarding and usage tracking. Budget: $5,000 a month, mostly time and content production, approved before launch. Timeline: Phase 1 (Jan 6 to early April 2027), the pilot lender runs the A/B test; Phase 2 (mid-April 2027), the case study, PR, outreach, and ads go live together; Phase 3 (May onward), we onboard new lenders and track LO activation and compliance at each one. Bad signal: demos and pilot requests but no signed contracts, which points to pricing, the value proposition, or the compliance veto, not lead volume. Likely decision: double down if lenders sign and their top producers activate; iterate on messaging if demos stall with buyers; pivot to compliance-led selling if the GC is the blocker.

Module 6

Individual Insights

Where the work was hardest, the moment my thinking changed, and what I learned.

Friction points

This product doesn't lend itself to ruthless, data-driven experimentation. There are too many variables, too few observations, time frames that are too long, and too many confounding factors. You want a beautiful, academic experiment, and you want to measure a business-value objective like revenue, but here both are truly impossible: a purchase loan can take 164 days from lead to funding, and only about 7% of referrals close. And in the real world, no CEO wants an academic study. They want it rolled out to everyone as quickly as possible so they can save time and money and give everyone the benefit.

Aha moment

Most of this felt natural. I'm a marketer, not a product person, but I've worked in product-led organizations, so the course mostly gave me more accurate language for what I was already doing. The moment that did shift my thinking came while designing the experiment: the perfect test, measured on revenue with a big, clean sample, was never possible for Nomi, because funded loans take up to 164 days and only about 7% of referrals close. The PM's job isn't to run the most academic experiment. It's to choose evidence that's good enough for the decision in front of you, be honest about what it can't prove, and move.

Key learnings

I enjoyed the rigor of going all the way through the process instead of going straight to a vibe-coded build. My thinking changed quite a bit along the way, about what I was creating, who I was solving for, and more. The forcing function of designing before building, and working through every one of these steps, shaped the outcome.
