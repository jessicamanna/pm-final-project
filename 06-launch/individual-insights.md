## Friction points

_The biggest technical or strategic challenge you faced while developing your product concept._

This product doesn't lend itself to ruthless, data-driven experimentation. There are too many variables, too few observations, time frames that are too long, and too many confounding factors. You want a beautiful, academic experiment, and you want to measure a business-value objective like revenue, but here both are truly impossible: a purchase loan can take 164 days from lead to funding, and only about 7% of referrals close. And in the real world, no CEO wants an academic study. They want it rolled out to everyone as quickly as possible so they can save time and money and give everyone the benefit.

## Key learnings

_A few surprising discoveries or insights you gained from the course overall._

I enjoyed the rigor of going all the way through the process instead of going straight to a vibe-coded build. My thinking changed quite a bit along the way, about what I was creating, who I was solving for, and more. The forcing function of designing before building, and working through every one of these steps, shaped the outcome.

## Aha! moment

_Your main "aha" moment during the project process._

Most of this felt natural. I'm a marketer, not a product person, but I've worked in product-led organizations, so the course mostly gave me more accurate language for what I was already doing. The moment that did shift my thinking came while designing the experiment: the perfect test, measured on revenue with a big, clean sample, was never possible for Nomi, because funded loans take up to 164 days and only about 7% of referrals close. The PM's job isn't to run the most academic experiment. It's to choose evidence that's good enough for the decision in front of you, be honest about what it can't prove, and move.
